Why the ninth line never fits

On a flat sheet, any connected drawing without crossings obeys Euler's formula: V − E + F = 2. Here there are 6 buildings and 9 lines, so a finished drawing would split the sheet into F = 5 regions.

Every line joins a utility to a house, so no region can be a triangle. Each region needs at least 4 lines around it, and each line borders 2 regions. That means 4F ≤ 2E, or 20 ≤ 18, which is false.

So you can always place 8 lines, and the ninth must cross something. Mathematicians call this graph K3,3, and it's one of the two basic shapes that can't be drawn flat. On the surface of a donut, though, it fits.
